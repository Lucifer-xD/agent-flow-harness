01

It plans its own work.

Before it touches a file, the agent decides what "done" means and lays out the steps — where you can see them and change them.

◎

Goals

The agent sets a goal from your request and keeps working until it's met — or tells you what's in the way. Set one yourself with /goal. Achieved goals are kept under the Goal button.

☰

A to-do list you can touch

Every step shows above the message box. Add, reorder or tick items yourself — the agent sees your edits on its next turn.

It asks instead of guessing

When a choice is really yours — which library, which design — it pauses and asks you multiple-choice questions, then carries on with your answer. Before a new app or site, it shows you design options A, B and C as live screens to pick from.

⌁

Picks up after a restart

Close the app mid-run, or lose power: when AFH starts again, interrupted runs resume by themselves.

❖

It remembers your project

How to run it, how to test it, the gotcha that cost an hour, the way you like things done: the agent saves what it learns to the project's memory, and every new thread starts knowing it. Open Memory beside the Goal button to add, edit or forget anything — or just say “remember…”, or type /remember. Your changes reach the agent on its very next step.

▣

Your computer, from your phone

The AFH app for Android and iPhone does two things. Chat with any model straight from your phone — OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, DeepSeek, xAI, Mistral or your own server — with your own keys, kept in the phone's secure storage. And remote control: your computers show who's online; tap one for its threads across every project, follow replies live step by step, approve and answer with a tap, send the next task, start a new thread or stop a run.

Connect a computer once with the Sign in button at the bottom of AFH's sidebar and approve it on agentflowharness.com — AFH never sees your password, and there's nothing to open on your router. The account is only for remote control; chat and AFH itself need none.

02

Four ways to work.

Pick a mode in the message box — or create your own agents with their own prompts, models and tools.

Build Ship it.

Reads, edits, runs and tests your project. Asks before anything risky, or runs on its own when you say so. The default.

Plan Think first.

Reads everything, writes nothing. You get a plan with the files it will touch before a line changes.

Delegate Fan out.

A lead model briefs helpers that work at the same time, each in its own lane or worktree, and checks what they bring back.

Red Team Break it before they do.

Audits your own project for exploitable weaknesses and reports each one with its severity, how it's exploited and the fix. It never edits code.

  • Auth and OTP flaws, weak sessions and tokens
  • Privilege escalation and admin bypass
  • Injection and exposed secrets
03

It splits the work.

A lead agent hands pieces to helpers that run side by side, then checks what they bring back.

⇶

Helpers in parallel

The built-in Helper is a copy of your own model for self-contained side work. Add your own subagents too, and watch any of them from the top bar: what the lead asked, and what came back.

◆

Mix models

Lead with your strongest model and delegate to a fast, cheap one. Each helper can use a different provider.

⑂

Git worktrees

Give a thread its own worktree — its own branch and folder — then merge it into your base branch when you're happy. Your main checkout stays clean.

⚡

Fast on many files

Reads, searches and helper tasks run in parallel, and old tool output is trimmed so long runs stay quick and cheap.

04

You watch it work.

Nothing happens behind a spinner. You see the thinking, the tools and the code as they happen.

⌕

A built-in browser, with DevTools

The agent opens pages in a side panel you watch live — your dev server, a docs site, the thing it just built. It clicks and types, runs JavaScript in the console, reads the console errors, inspects every network request and reads the source files the page loaded. So it debugs what it builds instead of guessing.

Click into its tab to take over; it waits until you hand it back. Sign-ins are kept between runs, and you can still choose an external Chrome or Edge.

◉

Live file pane

Tool arguments stream as they're generated, so code appears character by character in the Live pane — not after the fact. Commands show their output live too, like a terminal.

⏱

Every step on a timeline

Thinking time, each tool call, tokens per second and how long each reply took — all in the conversation.

✎

Errors caught after edits

After the agent edits a file, AFH checks it for errors and hands the results straight back, so broken code is fixed on the spot.

🎙

Images and voice

Paste or drop screenshots and files into the message box. Press the mic and speak — your words are typed in for you (with a Groq or OpenAI speech key).

05

You stay in control.

Decide what it may do alone, stop it at any moment, and undo anything — files included.

Rewind anything

"Undo to here" rewinds the thread and the files on disk to just before a message, and puts your message back in the box to edit and resend. Or fork the thread from any point.

⚑

Hooks

Run your own commands before or after any tool, or when the agent stops. Exit with code 2 to block a tool call — or to make the agent keep going.

$

Cost you can see

Tokens and cost per thread, budgets that stop a run, and prompt caching on by default for Anthropic models. A usage meter in the message box shows how much of your plan is left, live.

06

Built for long sessions.

Jobs that take hours, threads with thousands of messages — AFH is made for them.

∞

Endless threads

When a conversation fills 40, 60 or 80% of the model's context, older parts are summarised in place. You can still read every message; the model reads the summary. Or type /compact.

▶

Background jobs

Dev servers, watchers and long builds run in the background while the agent keeps working. See them with /jobs.

⏲

Schedules

"Every morning, update dependencies and run the tests." Run a prompt every few minutes, hourly, daily, on weekdays or weekly — each run is a new thread you can read. Jobs run while AFH is open.

⇄

Bring your threads

Import threads from OpenCode or Zadloop, and your OpenCode keys, endpoints and MCP servers in one click. Export any thread as JSON or Markdown.

Private by design

Yours, on your PC.

AFH runs and stores everything on your computer. No account needed and no telemetry. The optional AFH account only connects your phone to your computer; it never stores your threads, files or keys.

  • Threads, settings and undo history live in ~/.afh on your PC.
  • API keys are stored only on this computer.
  • Your code goes only to the model providers you choose.
  • The only other calls: the update check, the model catalogue refresh, and web search or voice if you turn them on.
